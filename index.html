What's on this map

Every place here is somewhere you can go outdoors in Connecticut. They're grouped by who owns and runs the land, because that's what decides whether you can walk in, what it's likely to be like, and who to ask when something's unclear.

What's deliberately left off

The rule: paid-but-open-to-anyone stays, exclusive-entry goes. A town beach charging non-residents is public. A members-only lake association is not.

Where the data comes from

CT DEEP's property records, the National Park Service, US Fish & Wildlife Service and Army Corps of Engineers, OpenStreetMap, USGS PAD-US, and the Connecticut Forest & Park Association's Blue-Blazed trail system. Elevation comes from USGS 3DEP. Everything is rebuilt from source rather than typed in by hand, so corrections belong upstream — though a handful of places OpenStreetMap has never heard of are added manually.
